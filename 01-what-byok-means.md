# What BYOK means

Bring your own key means you connect a tool to a provider using a key you got yourself, rather than paying the tool to include access. You deal with the provider directly, and you decide how much to spend. It trades a little setup effort for lower cost and more control.

## The everyday version

Imagine a tool that summarizes documents using an AI model. There are two ways it can work. The tool can include the AI and charge you a bundled price that covers its markup. Or the tool can ask you to bring your own key, meaning you sign up with the AI provider yourself, get a key, and paste it in. With your own key, the tool runs on the AI but the provider bills you at its own rates, usually for less than a bundled price.

## When BYOK is worth it

Bringing your own key tends to make sense when you use a tool enough that provider pricing beats a bundled subscription, when you want to control exactly what you spend, or when you want your usage and data tied to your own provider account rather than the tool's. It makes less sense when you use something rarely, because then the setup effort is not worth the small saving.

## What you take on

In exchange for the savings, you take on three responsibilities: getting the key, keeping it safe, and watching the cost. None of these require coding, but all three matter. A key you leak can be used by someone else on your bill. A key with no spending limit can run up more than you expected. The rest of this guide is about handling those three things calmly.

## The mindset

Think of an API key like a credit card number for a single service. You would not paste your card number into random places or leave it in a public document, and you would want a limit on it. The same care, applied to a key, is most of what safe BYOK use comes down to.
