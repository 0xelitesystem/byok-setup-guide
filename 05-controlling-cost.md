# Controlling cost

With your own key, the provider bills you for usage, so the way to avoid a surprise is to set limits at the provider before you use the key much. Most providers give you the controls; this section is about turning them on.

## Set a spending limit at the provider

The most important control is a hard spending limit on your provider account. Most providers let you cap how much you can be charged in a period. Set this to an amount you are comfortable with before you connect any tool. With a cap in place, the worst case is that usage stops when the cap is reached, not that the bill keeps climbing. This one setting removes most of the fear.

## Turn on usage alerts

Many providers can email you when your usage passes a threshold you set, for example at half and near your cap. Turn these on. Alerts give you warning before you reach the limit, so you can decide whether to raise the cap or slow down, rather than finding out after the fact. A cap plus alerts is the basic safety net.

## Understand what drives cost

Providers usually charge by how much you use, not a flat fee, so cost scales with activity. Before running a tool heavily, get a rough sense of what a typical action costs and multiply by how often you will do it. You do not need an exact figure, just enough to know whether your planned use is cents or a meaningful amount. The provider's pricing page and your usage dashboard are where you check.

## Start small and watch the meter

When you first connect a key, use the tool lightly and watch your provider usage dashboard to see what the activity actually costs. Real usage tells you more than any estimate. Once you have seen a day or two of normal use against the meter, you will know whether your cap and habits are right, and you can adjust with confidence.

## Cancel keys for tools you stop using

A key left active in a tool you no longer use is an open door for cost and risk with no benefit. When you stop using a tool, cancel its key at the provider. Because you made one key per tool and kept a map of where they are, this is a quick, clean action that closes the exposure completely.

## Keep BYOK in proportion

BYOK saves money when your usage justifies the setup, but the savings are not worth losing sleep over a runaway bill. The cap is what lets you relax. Once a sensible spending limit and alerts are in place, you can use your tools freely inside a boundary you chose, which is the whole point of controlling cost rather than avoiding it.
